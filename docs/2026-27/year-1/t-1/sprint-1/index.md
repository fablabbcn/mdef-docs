## Syllabus

This sprint challenges conventional notions of home, borders, and inhabitation by asking who—and what—has the right to occupy, move through, care for, and belong to a territory. Domesticity is approached not as a fixed architectural condition, but as a political, material, and multispecies construction shaped by labour, mobility, displacement, tourism, infrastructures, and unequal access to land.

Situated in the Parc Agrari and Delta del Llobregat, the sprint engages with a territory where agricultural resistance, airport expansion, tourist mobility, urban pressure, ecological protection, and multispecies habitats coexist and conflict. Students will approach this context through first-person research, direct encounters, and situated making, critically questioning their own position as temporary visitors and designers intervening in an already inhabited territory.

Through the operations of inflating, tensioning, and wrapping, students will experiment with textile waste, technical fabrics, and locally sourced materials to develop 1:1 spatial interventions that negotiate existing borders. Working between multiple scales, they will explore how to negotiate protection, permeability, hospitality, mobility, coexistence, etc;  Rather than producing autonomous objects, prototypes will act as material interfaces between bodies, infrastructures, environmental forces and politics, testing how temporary architectures can create friction with (and possibly propose alternative relationships with) the conditions that define a border.

The sprint combines first person 1:1 prototyping, pattern-making, digital fabrication, basic sewing, and situated material experimentation. Explore introduces textiles as material and structural systems through direct experimentation with folding, inflation, tension, suspension and wrapping. Build translates these experiments into full-scale prototypes through pattern development, projection, cutting, sewing, joining and structural testing. Digital tools are introduced where they support material exploration, allowing students to move iteratively between geometry, fabrication and embodied testing.

The course will culminate with a live implementation in El Prat, where prototypes are installed, inhabited, tested, and negotiated in relation to the territory. Installation itself becomes a research method: a way of testing how alternative architectures might produce temporary forms of belonging, encounter, care, and resistance.

### Learning Objectives

- Work through embodied methodologies and research-through-design.
- Test hypotheses in the field through direct interaction, situated observation and contextual prototyping.
- Translate territorial conditions into context-responsive architectural propositions.
- Explore relationships between body, space, climate, mobility, protection and inhabitation.
- Develop full-scale prototypes through iterative material experimentation.
- Understand the basic behaviour of textile, tensile and inflatable systems.
- Apply pattern-making, sewing and digital fabrication as tools for design development.
- Critically evaluate prototypes through their installation, use and interaction with a specific site.

## Design Brief

### Negotiate a 1:1 border

Conceive, prototype and implement a direct action that enters into a critical friction with the existing conditions of a border. The intervention does not need to eliminate or solve a border, instead, it should make explicit its intrinsic conditions, excite it and open it for other possible futures.

### Design requirements/ The intervention should:

- Situated and site-specific
- First person perspective and embodied
- Designed, fabricated and tested as a 1:1 prototype
- Temporary and reversible, leaving no permanent damage to the site.
- Engage critically with the border’s multiple levels of operation: material, infrastructural, political, ecological, social, legal, or symbolic.
- Consider the bodies, species, infrastructures, and other agents already inhabiting or negotiating the site
- Don’t design an object to place at the border. Design an action, triggered by the prototype, which problematizes and enters in critical friction with the different levels of operativity of the border

### Outcome

A 1:1 situated prototype, activated on site and documented in its real context, demonstrating the new triggered relationships between bodies, action, politics and territory at the selected border.

## Assessment and Submission Policy

- **Assignment Deadline:** All course deliverables must be submitted by Friday at 00:00 (midnight) of the last week of each Sprint.
- **Grading:** Faculty will publish grades by Friday at 00:00 (midnight) of the following week.
- **Resubmission:** Students who do not achieve a passing grade will have one additional week to resubmit their work. The resubmission deadline is Friday at 00:00 (midnight), two weeks after the original submission deadline. The maximum grade that can be awarded for a resubmitted assignment is 5.0 (Pass).
- **Attendance:** Students who are absent from more than 25% of the classes within a Sprint (4-week period) without valid justification will automatically fail the Sprint. This corresponds to more than three missed sessions.
- **Failure to Pass:** Students who do not submit their work by the resubmission deadline, or whose resubmitted work does not achieve the minimum passing grade of 5.0, will fail the Sprint.
- **Consequences of Failing:** Failing a Sprint means the course is not successfully completed. Students who fail a Sprint will not successfully complete the programme and will therefore not be eligible to receive the diploma.
