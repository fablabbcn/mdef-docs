## Schedule

=== "19/10"

    Èlia and Sara / Torres Bagó

    **Exercise 1 – Fly: Air, Structure and Balance:**
    
    The first day will introduce participants to Torres Bagó’s practice, beginning with an overview of selected projects and concluding with examples that connect more directly to the workshop. The three-day structure will then be introduced, together with the central principle of the workshop: exploring three different physical conditions through the transformation of the same geometry.
    
    Participants will work in groups of three. Each group will choose a two-dimensional geometry, working within maximum dimensions of 1 × 1 m. This same geometry will accompany the group throughout the three days, changing according to the material and physical requirements of each exercise.
    
    The first exercise will investigate how a flat geometry can become a lightweight structure capable of interacting with air. Taking the functioning of a kite as a reference, each group will work with paper, plastic, thread, thin branches and reeds to explore where structure is required, where flexibility is necessary, how tensions are distributed, and how weight and balance affect behaviour.
    
    One participant from each group will collect reeds and small branches around Cal Negre to incorporate into the structure. The exercise will involve repeated testing and adjustment, with particular attention to weight, balance, flexibility, reinforcement, connections between membrane and structure, and behaviour in relation to air.
    
    Each group will photographically document both the construction process and the final result.


=== "20/10"

    Èlia and Sara / Torres Bagó

    **Exercise 2 – Cover: Water, Membranes and Structure:** 
    
    The second day will begin from exactly the same geometry developed during the first exercise. Each group will transform it into a small fixed canopy or umbrella, without an opening or closing mechanism.
    
    The geometry will again move from plane to volume, but this time construction decisions will be conditioned by the movement of water. Participants will work primarily with plastic, exploring different ways of folding, joining and tensioning the membrane. Techniques may include heat sealing with an iron, double-sided tape, hot glue and other joining systems.
    
    The objective is to understand how the geometry of the membrane determines the path of water and how the structure needs to respond to and support that form. Participants will investigate slopes, the transition from two-dimensional to three-dimensional geometry, the minimum structure required to support the canopy, the relationship between membrane and rods, and the distribution of tension and support points.
    
    The exercise will continue the iterative process of making, testing and adapting established on the first day. Each group will again document both the process and the completed construction.


=== "21/10"

     Èlia and Sara / Torres Bagó

    **Exercise 3 – Tension: Wind, Porosity and Collective Structure:** 
    
    On the third day, each group will work once again with the same geometry, but the resulting piece will no longer function independently. Instead, each group will construct one fragment of a collective textile wall.
    
    The exercise will investigate the relationship between a vertical textile surface and wind. Because a continuous surface creates considerable resistance to airflow, participants will explore openings, perforations, cuts, porosity, deformation and tension as ways of allowing air to pass through the structure while maintaining its condition as a vertical boundary.
    
    Particular attention will be given to joining systems, tension, suspended structures, repetition, and the way an individual component becomes part of a larger collective construction. The final installation will emerge from the combination of all the geometries developed by the different groups during the workshop.
    
    The workshop will conclude with the assembly of the collective structure and a review of the three exercises. The photographic documentation produced throughout the workshop will be used to compare the different material, structural and construction solutions developed through the processes of flying, covering and tensioning.
     
     
## Evaluation Criteria

**1. Critical and situated technological literacy**

Decoding the foundations of contemporary technology, including systems thinking, infrastructures and architectures, political economy, IP models and planned obsolescence, to develop a critical position that informs design practice.

**2. Exploration and reframing of emergent and alternative technologies**

Engagement with emergent technologies from research and industry alongside alternative ones, including deprecated, marginal, vernacular and analogue technologies, considering their narratives, applications and implementation, and recombining them to open new design possibilities.

**3. Collaborative Practice**

The contract documents clearly how knowledge, references, tools or skills were sourced, shared or developed with others, demonstrating an ability to learn from existing practices and contribute findings back to the group.
