    Exercise for 14/10:

    Identify and document (images, audiovisual recordings, drawings, short texts…) 10 different borders by:

    - **Infrastructure**: what material or immaterial systems produce and sustain the border? 
    - **Agents**: who produces and sustains the border, who wants or needs to cross it, who is affected by it, who benefits from it? Who is excluded/ included by it?
    - **Conditions**: under what environmental, spatial, economic, legal, or social conditions does the border operate?
    - **Temporality**: is it temporary, permanent, seasonal, porous, shifting?
    - **Politics**: What forms of power, ownership, regulation, privilege, extraction, protection, or resistance are embedded in the border?
    - **Inbetweeness**: thresholds, margins, buffer zones, residual spaces, overlaps, ambiguities, territories of nobody/ non-belonging
    - **Inhabitants**: Which human and more-than-human bodies inhabit, depend on, cross, avoid, or transform the border?
    - **Actions**: to cross/ to occupy/ to conquer/ to protect/ to surveil/ to trace/ to hide/ to invisibilize/ to extract/ to care/ to exclude/ etc.

    **Important!**: A border should not be understood only as a physical boundary, but as a political, social and ecological condition, that separates, filters, regulates, controls, excludes or connects, and politicizes the act of belonging and inhabiting. Borders may be spatial, ecological, political, infrastructural, social, invisible, etc…

## Evaluation Criteria

Assessment will be based exclusively on the evidence included in the group Declaration of Commitments. This includes the documentation of physical prototypes, experiments, iterations and relevant design decisions. Any work not represented in the contract cannot be considered as part of the evaluation.

**1. Critical Briefing (Hypothesis) and Situatedness**

Develop a clear, situated hypothesis that articulates the project's initial position, identifies a relevant question or tension, and establishes a meaningful relationship with the specific conditions of the context.


**2. Methodological Commitment**

Define and justify a research methodology aligned with the project's hypothesis, context, and objectives. Establish a clear approach to inquiry, identifying the methods, practices, and forms of engagement through which knowledge will be generated and tested.

**3. Conceptual Coherence and Development**

Develop a coherent conceptual framework that connects the project's hypothesis, research, and methodological approach. Articulate the key ideas, relationships, and assumptions underpinning the project, and demonstrate how these can evolve through critical reflection and experimentation.
