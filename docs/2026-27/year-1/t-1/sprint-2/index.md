## Syllabus

This sprint questions the boundaries between the desirable and the monstrous by exploring how bodies, technologies, desires, and imaginaries are shaped through politics of pleasure and affect. Students investigate how contemporary societies produce and regulate desires by building stereotypes of what is considered attractive, excessive, abnormal, or mysterious, examining the political and cultural forces behind these constructions. We will research countercultural forms of celebration and hedonism in order to design novel practices of embodied resistance based on alterity and celebrated monstruosity.

Students will design and prototype wearable artifacts enacted through party-like relational formats: performative situations through collective rituals, embodied experimentation, and experimental protocols.We will explore how wearables can operate as tools for world-building, based on joy, excess, and pleasure as acts of cultural and political resistance.

Explore introduces wearable technologies as material, sensory and cultural interfaces between bodies and their environments. Through DIY sensors, soft materials, electronics and bodily experimentation, students will investigate how movement, perspiration, contact, pressure or flexion can become computational inputs and how these measurements construct particular representations of the body.

Build translates these experiments into functional body-worn devices. Each student develops a wearable combining one bodily input, a portable computational system and an individual or collective output. Through physical prototyping, programming and networked interaction, students examine technology not as a neutral layer applied to the body, but as an active mediator of perception, behaviour, agency and social relations.

The resulting artefacts will be activated collectively through a performative situation in which bodies, devices, sound and images become part of a shared system. The final event becomes both a celebration and a critical experiment in how technological bodies can sense, affect and transform one another.

### Learning Objectives

- Critically analyse cultural constructions of bodies, desire, pleasure, normality and monstrosity.
- Explore embodied and performative methods as forms of design research.
- Understand fundamental principles of wearable technologies, soft sensors and physical computing.
- Construct simple DIY sensors capable of translating bodily or physical phenomena into data.
- Programme basic relationships between sensor inputs, computation and digital outputs.
- Integrate electronics, power and connectivity into a wearable artefact through iterative physical prototyping.
- Critically interrogate what bodily data measures, represents and excludes.
- Design interactions that operate between individual bodies and collective technological systems.
- Test a prototype through performance, embodied use and collective activation.

## Design Brief

### Perform joy

Design a wearable-mediated ritual to join a party. Create a relational situation where bodies can temporarily become others. Approach the party as a political machine for pleasure: a space where stereotypes and normative behaviours are reproduced, questioned, exaggerated, summoned, disrupted, or collapsed. Through hedonism, enchantment, ritual, and embodied transformation, explore how bodies can perform alternative identities, desires, relationships, and ways of joy and pleasure. Use monstrosity and hedonism as critical design material to imagine and enact alternative forms of individual and collective identity.

### Design Requirements

The intervention should:

- 1:1 wearable or body-mediated prototype
- Operate through ritual, pleasure, enchantment and collective celebration
- Transform the behaviour, perception, interactions between bodies
- Produce a relational situation, not simple an object or costume
- Engage critically with music and entertainment culture
- Intercept an existing party with your prototype, so your intervention can propose alternative forms of individual and/ or collective delight
- Don’t design a costume for a party. Design a prototype that makes another kind of party possible.

### Outcome

A 1:1 wearable-mediated ritual, collectively activated at an existing party as a temporary experiment in monstruosity, pleasure and joy.

## Assessment and Submission Policy

- Assignment Deadline: All course deliverables must be submitted by Friday at 00:00 (midnight) of the last week of each Sprint.
- Grading: Faculty will publish grades by Friday at 00:00 (midnight) of the following week.
- Resubmission: Students who do not achieve a passing grade will have one additional week to resubmit their work. The resubmission deadline is Friday at 00:00 (midnight), two weeks after the original submission deadline. The maximum grade that can be awarded for a resubmitted assignment is 5.0 (Pass).
- Attendance: Students who are absent from more than 25% of the classes within a Sprint (4-week period) without valid justification will automatically fail the Sprint. This corresponds to more than three missed sessions.
- Failure to Pass: Students who do not submit their work by the resubmission deadline, or whose resubmitted work does not achieve the minimum passing grade of 5.0, will fail the Sprint.
- Consequences of Failing: Failing a Sprint means the course is not successfully completed. Students who fail a Sprint will not successfully complete the programme and will therefore not be eligible to receive the diploma.
- No Additional Resubmission Opportunities: No further resubmission or recovery opportunities will be offered beyond the single resubmission described above.
