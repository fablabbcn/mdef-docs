## Syllabus

The Thesis Project course is designed to support and guide students through the process of developing their final design projects during the second year of the Master in Design for Emergent Futures. Throughout the academic year, students will engage in three key phases: Situate (Situated Context + Research question + Hypothesis of innovation), Explore+Build (Method as practice + Interventions + Case Studies/Evidence + Project Proposition) and Reflect (Dissemination + Contribution + Continuity), each building on the prior to ensure that the project is well-researched, contextually grounded, and capable of being scaled for real-world impact. The course emphasizes interdisciplinary research, design methodologies, field engagement, and the creation of sustainable business models.

By the end of the course, students will have created a fully developed, scalable, and contextually situated project that reflects their ability to address complex societal challenges with innovative, emergent design solutions.

## Schedule

=== "T1: Situate"

    In the first term, students will focus on conducting research and establishing the scientific background of their projects. They will delve into relevant theories, methodologies, and frameworks to inform their design process. Students will gain a solid understanding of the context and theoretical foundations of their projects, as well as situating their projects within a specific community and context.

    **Learning Objectives**

    - Develop advanced research skills to investigate and establish the scientific background of design projects, specifically focusing on the integration of emerging technologies and their impact on societal, cultural, and environmental contexts in order to develop their projects and practices as Alternative Presents to current challenges.
    - Apply theoretical frameworks and methodologies to inform the design process and address complex challenges in emergent futures, with a particular emphasis on the ethical and sustainable integration of emerging technologies, situating their practices from a first-person perspective critically assessing scalable socio-technical systems.
    - Gain an understanding of the social, cultural, environmental, political, and economical aspects that influence design implementation within specific communities and contexts, considering the potential implications and effects of emerging technologies on these factors.

    **Schedule**

    - Session 0: Thursday 08/10 10:00 - 11:00
    - Session 1: Monday 26/10 10:00-13:00 (Individual presentation / collective feedback)
    - Session 2: Monday 16/11 10:00-13:00 (Individual reviews)
    - Session 3: Monday 30/11 15:00-18:00 (Individual reviews)
    - Session 4: Monday 14/12 10:00-13:00 (Final presentation - Jury)

    **Deliverables/Outputs**

    1. Project Title & Position
        - Project title
        - Subtitle as a clear position or claim (not a theme)
        - One-sentence statement of what the project actively does
    2. Situated Context
        - Specific context(s) the project operates in (place, community, system, scale)
        - Why this context matters now
        - Weak signals as they appear in the field, not abstract trends
    3. Research Question and hypothesis of innovation (Project Stance)
        - Refined research question after term 1
        - What the project takes a stand on (what it supports, resists, reframes)
        - State of the art and hypothesis of innovation (existing practitioners and theories that you align to)

    **Evaluation Criteria**

    - Situatedness (20%)
    - Critical commitment (20%)
    - Quality of formalization (30%)
    - Viability of the proposal (15%)
    - Participation (15%)

=== "T2: Validation"

    During the second term, students will explore the social, cultural, environmental, political, and economical aspects that influence the development and implementation of their designs. Students will gain insights into the needs, aspirations, and challenges of the community they aim to serve.

    **Learning Objectives**

    - Build a solid foundation for engagement by critically analyzing the feasibility and relevance of planned interventions and subsequent analyses within a specific context.
    - Promote design interventions encompassing field research and participatory methods to gain insights into the needs, aspirations, and challenges in the context exploring how these can be leveraged to create positive social impact.

    **Schedule**

    - Session 1: TBC
    - Session 2: TBC
    - Session 3: TBC
    - Session 4: TBC

    **Deliverables/Outputs**

    4. Method as Practice
        - How research is conducted through action
        - First-person position (your role, implication, responsibility)
        - Situated actions already carried out
        - Tools and methods only as they relate to decisions made

    5. Interventions
        - Description of 1–3 concrete interventions
        - What was tested, activated, or disrupted
        - What changed because of each intervention (behaviours, relations, narratives)

    6. Case Studies / Evidence
        - Case studies produced by the project or critically engaged with
        - What each case reveals or proves
        - How cases informed iterations or shifts in the project

    7. Project Proposition (Current State)
        - The project as it exists now (not future vision)
        - System, artifact, protocol, or strategy
        - What makes this proposition specific and non-generic

    **Evaluation Criteria**

    - Methodological development (20%)
    - Experimentation (20%)
    - Intervention quality (situatedness, coherence with critical statement and strategy) (30%)
    - Formalization and documentation (20%)
    - Participation (10%)

=== "T3: Dissemination. Scaling, Contributing and Sharing"

    In the final term, students will work on the scalability model of their projects. They will explore strategies for scaling up their designs to reach a wider audience and have a greater impact. Additionally, students will develop sustainability and viability strategies for their projects. They will consider factors such as funding, partnerships, and distribution to create a comprehensive plan for implementing their designs.

    **Learning Objectives**

    - Enable students to create actionable plans for scaling their projects, considering contextual, technical, and community factors to develop a scalable implementation strategy.
    - Develop a communication strategy to disseminate their projects.
    - Guide students in designing a sustainable model integrating funding strategies, partnerships, and distribution mechanisms into their projects, ensuring long-term viability and resilience of their solutions.

### T2 & T3 - Individual Mentoring

From the second trimester onwards, each student will have access to individual mentoring sessions with an external mentor, selected according to the specific area of expertise relevant to their Thesis Project.

Each student will receive a total of 3 hours of individual mentoring distributed across the second and third trimesters. These sessions are intended to provide specialised guidance, feedback, and support in the development of the student’s project.

External mentors may be proposed by the students and/or assigned by the Thesis Project faculty, depending on the needs and specific focus of each project.
