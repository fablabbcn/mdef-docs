## Syllabus

### Hacking Urban Mobility | From Embodied Experience and Data to Urban Intervention

How can we understand urban mobility beyond transport systems and conventional planning? And how can technology help us reveal, measure and intervene in the conditions that shape how different bodies move through the city?

This course is part of JustMOBI, a European Erasmus+ project bringing together five European institutions working on new approaches to mobility education. Alongside the course content, students will have the opportunity to connect with students, faculty and professionals from other institutions and countries, opening up possibilities for knowledge exchange, networking and interdisciplinary collaboration.

This course explores urban mobility through embodied experience, citizen science, data and digital fabrication. Students investigate everyday mobility in Barcelona, identifying barriers and overlooked needs through situated observation and fieldwork.

The course follows a process from experience to hypothesis, data and physical intervention. Students formulate a research question and use appropriate technological tools — such as sensors, Smart Citizen Kits, Arduino, phone sensors, GPS, cameras or manual data collection — to generate and analyse their own evidence. They connect this situated data with open urban datasets to understand broader patterns.

The final stage translates research findings into a physical intervention in public space. Students experiment with digital fabrication, basic electronics, physical interfaces and prototyping to adapt or “hack” an existing urban element such as a sign, bench, bike rack or other street infrastructure.

The course combines critical urban research with hands-on technological experimentation and making, with gender, care and accessibility embedded throughout the process.

As part of the JustMOBI project, some students may also participate in activities connecting the course to the wider European network, including:

- Hybrid Students' Presentations (Autumn 2027): online presentations bringing together students from IAAC, SWPS and the University of Limerick with students, faculty, professionals and experts from other institutions.
- Urban Mobility Innovation Hackathon (AY 2027–28): an interdisciplinary activity where students develop innovative proposals addressing urban mobility challenges.
- Students' Mentorship Programme (AY 2027–28): a professional mentoring and networking programme for 12 students selected from the Hackathon, including research guidance and career development.
- Final European Symposium in Barcelona (Spring–Summer 2028): the final project event, bringing together selected students, faculty, professionals and experts to present and discuss projects developed through the programme.

These activities extend the course beyond the classroom, connecting students to a wider European community working across design, technology, research and urban mobility.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the course students will be able to:

- Critically examine urban mobility systems from social, cultural and inclusive perspectives.
- Identify urban barriers through embodied experience, observation and mapping.
- Formulate a specific and testable mobility-related hypothesis.
- Select and use appropriate sensors, data-capture tools and citizen-science methods.
- Collect, analyse and interpret situated data and connect it with open urban datasets.
- Understand basic ethical considerations around data collection in public space.
- Translate research findings into physical interventions using prototyping, digital fabrication and basic electronics.
- Test and critically evaluate an intervention in the real urban environment.

## Exercise 

Students work in groups to investigate a mobility-related question in Barcelona.

They begin with embodied observation and fieldwork, identify a specific urban condition and formulate a testable hypothesis. They then design a data-capture strategy, collect and analyse evidence using appropriate technological tools, and connect their findings with larger urban datasets.

Finally, they translate their findings into a physical intervention in public space, using prototyping and fabrication to adapt, hack or reprogram an existing urban element.

The intervention should respond to evidence generated during the course and consider who it serves, who it may overlook and what unintended consequences it could produce.

- Mobility research topic and mapping
- Fieldwork and site documentation

- Argued conclusion

### Evaluation, Assessment, and Final Grade

Students shall be evaluated according to the following:

- Critical research and quality of observation
- Formulation and development of the hypothesis
- Appropriate use of data and technology
- Analysis and interpretation of evidence
- Technical and conceptual development of the intervention
- Inclusion and accessibility
- Documentation and communication
- Participation and project development
